# A-Leakage-Free-Pipeline-with-Conformal-Uncertai
**(Physics-AI Multi-Task Learning for Residential Energy Prediction under Climate Extremes)**

Machine learning has become the default tool for residential energy prediction. Yet three problems persist across the literature.

First, leakage. Training and testing pipelines are seldom audited systematically; when they are, the check is typically a one-time manual scan rather than a reproducible protocol. Second, overconfidence. Most models report a single R$^2$ value and stop there, offering no guidance on how wrong any individual prediction might be. For retrofit programmes where misallocated capital wastes public funds, this is a policy risk, not merely a statistical gap. Third, physical inconsistency. End-use disaggregation is usually treated as an independent post-processing step, ignoring the first-law constraint that end-use sums cannot exceed total site energy.

We do not claim an R^2 record. On cross-sectional survey data, unobserved occupant behaviour sets an irreducible noise ceiling. Instead, we offer a prediction pipeline that is leakage-audited, uncertainty-calibrated, and climate-generalisable --- the kind of methodological trustworthiness that deployment-ready energy policy requires.
